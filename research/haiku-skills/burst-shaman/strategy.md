## Win condition and role

Earn early damage with cheap, independently useful minions, then finish with Doomhammer and spells. Against fast decks, use those same damage cards to stabilize before racing. Against control, stop spending every attack on replaceable minions once a realistic lethal window exists. This deck can finish after a board clear, but cannot ignore Taunts, healing, weapon destruction or its own health.

Relative to existing midrange Shaman, this removes the Bloodlust/Flametongue reliance and adds early attackers, two Lava Bursts and a second Doomhammer. Harvest Golem bridges the early board; Loot Hoarder replaces some missing Azure Drake refill. Two weapons improve access and recovery after Ooze, but drawing both early is a liability. Al'Akir is an independent finisher and emergency defensive play, not a mandatory combo to hoard.

## Mulligan and turn planning

Seek Squire/Worgen and a playable follow-up. Keep Loot Hoarder; keep Golem or Unbound with a curve. Bolt is useful against early threats. Return expensive finishers and usually Lava Burst. Feral Spirit is useful only when its two locked crystals do not ruin the following turn. Do not keep a slow Mana Tide merely because the deck needs draw.

Separate damage that must remove threats from damage that can end the game. Calculate next turn's AVAILABLE mana after Overload before committing. A turn-five Doomhammer leaves only four usable crystals on turn six. Rockbiter costs TWO here. Never plan using historical one-mana Rockbiter or three-mana Hex.

## Concrete damage lines

- Doomhammer plus two Rockbiters costs nine mana from hand and gives two eight-damage swings: 16 damage with an unfrozen hero, two attacks available and an unobstructed target. Apply both buffs BEFORE either swing. Having attacked with another weapon can reduce available swings; use the current legal state.
- An already equipped Doomhammer plus one Rockbiter gives ten weapon damage for two mana. Double Rockbiter plus double Lava Burst costs ten usable mana and totals 26 damage, before armor, Taunts or other effects. This is a conditional ceiling, not the deck's routine plan; prior Overload can prevent it.
- Al'Akir plus one Rockbiter costs ten and delivers 12 attack damage if both attacks can connect. Al'Akir plus TWO Rockbiters costs twelve and is not an ordinary ten-mana line.
- Thalnos plus two Lava Bursts costs eight and deals 12 spell damage. Thalnos plus both Bolts and both Bursts costs ten and deals 20, but requires five specific cards. Do not hoard the entire package while losing the board.
- Earth Shock silences before damage: use it to remove Tirion's shield, Taunt and deathrattle, then attack past him. Hex removes the original deathrattle but leaves a 0/1 TAUNT that must still be cleared. Do not treat Hex alone as opening face attacks.

Use Mana Tide when the opponent cannot remove it cheaply, or when one immediate end-turn draw plus forcing an answer is already worthwhile. Do not trade away protected attackers just to shelter a speculative second draw.

## Matchups, weak slots and falsification

Control Paladin: pressure before healing, protect a burst window, and track revealed Ooze and Taunts. Do not spend eight turns chipping into repeated healing without accumulating a finishing hand. Zoo/aggro Paladin: early board matters more than saving every Bolt; Storm and Wolves buy time, but using health as weapon removal is costly. Mage: freezing the hero stops Doomhammer; preserve spell damage as an alternative and respect burn range.

Main risks are scarce healing, conditional refill, Overload mistakes and weapon answers. One Storm may be insufficient against wide decks. First isolated variants: second Doomhammer → second Storm if repeated wide-board losses; Al'Akir → second Farseer if expensive cards strand while dying; one Hex → second Earth Shock only if silence-access failures dominate and large non-Taunt threats are manageable. Keep these as separate comparisons.

For evaluation, record whether losses occur before Doomhammer, after an answered weapon, after a missed burst, or because healing outruns total damage. A loss with unused damage is not automatically a deck-construction failure.
